# <span class="ms-outline">DV01.</span>

## Definition

**DV01** (Dollar Value of 01, also called "PV01" or "BPV", Basis Point Value) measures the change in **money value** (P&L) of a bond position for a **1 basis point (1 bp = 0.01%)** move in yield.

It is the "in real euros/dollars" version of Modified Duration, which gives a % price change for a 1% = 100 bps move.

## The formula linking DV01 and Duration

`DV01 ≈ Modified Duration × Price × 0.0001`

The 0.0001 comes from the fact that 1 bp = 0.0001 in decimal. You can think of DV01 as Modified Duration, but calibrated for a move 100 times smaller, and expressed in money rather than in %.

## Full worked example

A bond position with a notional of €10M, Modified Duration = 7, price = 100 (at par).

`DV01 ≈ 7 × €10,000,000 × 0.0001 = €7,000`

So DV01 = €7,000.

- Yield **+1 bp** → P&L **≈ −€7,000**
- Yield **−1 bp** → P&L **≈ +€7,000**

For a larger move, you simply multiply. If the yield rises by 20 bps, P&L ≈ −€7,000 × 20 = −€140,000 (linear approximation, see the limit below).

## Why traders use DV01 rather than Modified Duration day to day

Because **P&L is counted in money, not in percent**. A trader running a book wants to know how much they make or lose in euros if rates move by 1 bp, and DV01 answers that question directly, with no extra conversion. It is also the unit used to **aggregate risk** across a whole portfolio. You add up the DV01 of each position to get the total DV01 of the book.

## The limit, convexity

DV01 and Modified Duration are **linear approximations**. They assume the price moves in proportion to the yield. But in reality, the price-yield relationship is **curved (convex)**, not a straight line.

In practice, for a small yield move (1-2 bps), the linear approximation (DV01) is very accurate. But for a **large** move (100+ bps), the actual loss or gain differs from what DV01 alone predicts, and that difference is called **convexity**.

The direction of the error matters. Convexity generally works in **favor** of the bondholder. When yields fall sharply, the price rises **more** than duration alone predicts. When yields rise sharply, the price falls **less** than predicted. That is why convexity is considered a **positive** feature to have in a bond portfolio.

Graphical analogy, if you plot price against yield, the real curve is bowed (convex), and the DV01/duration line is just the **tangent** to that curve at one point. The further you move from that point, the bigger the gap between the tangent and the real curve.

<div class="ms-takeaway" markdown>

## Key takeaway

- DV01 turns rate sensitivity into **real money**, the number one risk unit on a fixed income trading floor.
- Valid for **small** yield moves only. For large moves, you also need to account for **convexity**, otherwise DV01 and Modified Duration become misleading.
